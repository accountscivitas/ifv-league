Team strength

⚠ This is a RECENCY rating and it will disagree with the all-time standings — that is the point of it, not a fault in it. A quarter of each team's distance from 1500 is given back every offseason, and measured against this league's own record that leaves a memory about two seasons deep: the current ratings correlate +0.98 with win rate over 2024–25 and only +0.46 with win rate over all fifteen seasons. So a franchise can sit near the top here on a losing career record, and a good career can sit at the bottom after two bad years. For the career, read the all-time table on the Overview. Elo also reads the RESULT and not the margin — a one-point win and a ninety-point win move the ladder identically — which is why the power rating below it reads only the scoring and ignores who won. Where the two disagree about a team, the disagreement is the finding. One caveat the league already knows: three of the twelve slots have no roster thread across the 2011–2012 seam, so their 2011 games were somebody else's team. After fourteen offseasons of regression that is worth about two points of rating.

The two side by side

Elo asks who won; the index asks who scored. Where a franchise sits higher on Elo than on scoring it has been winning more than its points bought, and where it sits lower it has been losing games it outscored — the same luck the all-play record measures, in continuous form. On the committed record the mean gap is 1.83 places and the largest is 6, so the two mostly agree about the league and the disagreements name somebody.
