Part 1 — 2024 rulebook, verbatim

2024 In Football Veritas

Official Rulebook

[Updated 08.2024]

Roster

Starters
  • Ten total: 1 QB, 2 RBs, 3 WR, 1 TE, 1 Flex (RB/WR/TE), 1 K, 1 DEF.
Bench
  • Seven bench spots, for a total of 17 active roster spots during the regular season
Draft Rules

During the draft, team may only draft 16 players (not 17).

Auction Draft

Budget
  • Budget = $225.
  • At the draft, you must always have at least $1 per remaining player needed.

Positions

  • You also must draft a full starting lineup (e.g., you must draft at least one kicker and at least one defense).

Nominations

Nomination order is the last place team (fewest points), followed by the champion, finalist, semifinalist with highest points, semifinalist with lower points, quarterfinalists in points order; remaining teams in points order.

Additional

  • Carryover of any excess auction money to the free agent money is prohibited.

The night of the auction, teams may drop players before the auction (and take the appropriate deduction). But teams not not drop players during the auction itself.

Keepers
Keepers: Contract-based system
  • After a player's initial year on your roster (called his "A year"), you can elect to keep him the following season at a $2 increase over the “A year” purchase price (his "B year").
  • After his B year, you have the option to sign the player a long-term contract (his “C year”).

The base contract price is the “A year” price.

For each year of the contract, the player’s salary must increase by $3 per year.

E.g., a two-year contract = $6 per year; three-year = $9 per year.

The maximum contract length is 5 years (meaning that, with the A and B years, the maximum you can keep a player is 7 years).

The additional funds above the base price can be distributed in any form over the life of the contract (i.e., you cannot go below the base).

  • During the contract period, if you cut a player, you lose half his salary (rounded up) at the auction each year until the contract expires. Basically, it’s “dead money.”
Keepers: Auction Purchases
  • Players purchased at the auction (and not dropped) can be kept.

Keepers: Free Agent Aquisitions

  • General
  • Free agent acquisitions can be kept, subject to the rules below:

Pricing:

For any player acquired through Free Agency (including players purchased at auction but then dropped), the player’s “A year” salary is subject to a $10 minimum.

For any player acquired through Free Agency who was also purchased at auction but then dropped, the player’s “A year” salary is the higher of the auction price and the free agent price, subject to a $10 minimum.

For any player acquired through Free Agency who was previously acquired through FA (by another team), the “A year” price is the final acquisition through FA.

For any player acquired through Free Agency who was previously acquired through FA (by you ), the “A year” price is the higher of the two acquisition prices through FA.

Ineligible players.

  • The following free agent acquisitions cannot be kept:
  • Acquisitions that occur after the end of the regular season (e.g., any acquisition on or after Thursday of Week 14, the first pickups in the playoffs)

Players in the final year of their contract (i.e., players in their final year who were dropped and then picked up).

Deadline to select Keepers
  • Two Mondays before the draft.

Failure to submit keepers on time results in reduction of $5 auction dollars per 24-hour period (all or part) late (e.g., 25 hours late = $10 deduction)

Example of Keeper Pricing
  • In the 2013 draft, I draft Eli Manning for $20 (his “A year”).
  • By two weeks before draft, I must select whether to keep Eli for his "B year," at $22. I choose to do so, meaning that my budget for the 2014 auction is now $203 ($225 - $22).
  • The following year, I must determine whether to sign Eli to a contract.

The salary for a one-year contract (2015) will be $23 ($20 base + $3 per year).

The average salary for a two-year contract (2015 & 2016) will be $26 ($20 base + $6 per year [$3 * 2 years]).

The base salary is $20 (the “A year” price).

The excess salary is $12 ($6 per year * 2 years).

I can distribute the excess salary ($12) above the $20 base any way that I choose.

For example, 2015 is $20 and 2016 is $32.

Or 2015 is $23 and 2016 is $29.

Or 2015 is $26 and 2016 is $26.

Using the $20/$32 example, let’s say that I keep Eli for the 2015 year, but prior to the 2016 auction, I drop him.

My auction budget for 2016 (the last remaining year on the contract) is decreased by half of his salary $32/2 = $16. So I’ll have a budget of only $204 ($225 - $16).

The average salary for a three-year contract (2015, 2016 & 2017) is $29 ($20 base + $9 per year [$3 * 3 years]).

The excess salary is $27 ($9 per year * 3 years).

Again, I can distribute the excess salary any way I choose.

For ease, let’s say that I use $29 for all three years.

Let’s say that I keep Eli for the 2015 year, but prior to the 2016 auction, I drop him.

My auction budget for 2016 and 2017 will be decreased by half, $15 ($29/2, rounded up). So I’ll have a budget of only $205 ($225 - $15) for both 2016 and 2017.

The average salary for a four-year contract (2015, 2016, 2017 & 2018) is $32 ($20 base + $12 per year [$3 * 4 years]).

The average salary for a five-year contract (2015, 2016, 2017, 2018 & 2019) is $35 ($20 base + $15 per year [$3 * 5 years]).

Free Agent Pickups During Season
Budget
  • $275, with no additional monies added for losses.

Order of Pickups

Prior to week 1, If there is a tie in bid price, the following tiebreakers apply:

Lowest order to finish in prior year

Week 2 and forward, if there is a tie in bid price, the following tiebreakers apply:

Least points

Worst record

Trading

Counteroffer Rule
  • The Counteroffer Rule continues in effect. Once a trade is accepted on the website, teams have 24 hours to counter. If a counteroffer is accepted, the original trading party may attempt to best that offer, as long as it falls within the 24 hour window.
Auction and Free Agent Dollar Trading
  • During the offseason and during the season, teams are permitted to transfer auction dollars for the upcoming season, and free agent dollars for the current (if during the season) and upcoming season.
Trade Deadline
  • All trades must be finalized by Sunday at 12:50p of Week 12 (prior to last set of divisional games). Given the counter-offer window, all trades must be accepted (so the counter-offer window begins) by the Saturday of Week 12, 12:49p ).

Divisions, Scheduling & Playoffs

Divisions
  • Teams will be placed into three divisions.
  • For all subsequent years, the top 4 playoff teams (e.g., the 4 semis team) are placed in one division; last 2 playoff teams and top 2 points in second division; and the final 4 teams in the third.
Scheduling
  • The regular season runs from Week 1 to Week 13. The playoffs run from Weeks 14 to Week 16.
  • During the regular season, each team plays the other 3 teams in his division twice, during the first 3 weeks of the regular season and the last 3 (6 total games). Each team then plays every other team once.
Playoffs
  • Qualification

The three division winners make the playoffs.

The next three highest points make the playoffs.

  • Seeding

The top two seeds: (1) most points during regular season (tie breaker based on record) and (2) best remaining division-winning record (tie breaker based on most points).

Next four seeds: remaining four teams, in order of most points during regular season

  • Playoff Schedule

Seeds #1 and #2 receive a bye the first week (Week 14).

In the playoffs, the highest seed gets to choose opponent. Selections are due by Thursday at 11a (1 hour before the pickup processing at 12p).

Dispute Resolution and Rule Changes

All disputes shall be resolved by the then current commissioners in their sole discretion. The commissioners must reach a consensus to resolve any disputes.

For the 2024-25 IFV season, the Commissioners are Ray and Peter.

If anyone wishes to be a Commissioner in a future year, they can nominate themselves in the offseason. If there are two or fewer candidates for Commissioner, then all those who wish to serve shall serve for a one-year term to be extended unless there are more than two candidates in a given year or they resign. If there are more than two candidates in a given year, an election via secret ballot shall be held at the draft and the top two voter receivers (one vote per ownership group) will be the new Commissioners. A Commissioner candidate may vote for themselves.

Any changes to the Rules shall be made in the offseason and shall require the vote of a simple majority (seven ownership groups).

Amendment 1 — Keeper cutoff for free-agent acquisitions

Free agents acquired after Sunday of Week 12 (the first divisional-playoff games) cannot be kept.

Supersedes Part 1's "Acquisitions that occur after the end of the regular season (e.g., any acquisition on or after Thursday of Week 14, the first pickups in the playoffs)."

Source: owner, 2026-08-24. This is why several teams carry fewer than 16 keeper-eligible players on the pre-keeper worksheet.

Implementation: ifv.rules.eligibility.is_keeper_eligible takes season_fa_cutoff_period; seasons.keeper_fa_cutoff stores it per season. Acquisition timestamps must be compared in league time — see Amendment note below.

Amendment 2 — Nomination-order tie-breaks sort descending

Where Part 1 says "quarterfinalists in points order; remaining teams in points order," "points order" means most points first (descending).

Source: owner, 2026-08-25.

Implementation: the deterministic nomination-order generator sorts descending. Full order: last place (fewest points) → champion → finalist → semifinalist with higher points → semifinalist with lower points → quarterfinalists descending by points → remaining teams descending by points.

Note: ESPN's stored nomination order for 2026 is wrong — it is a straight reverse-final-rank sort that places the champion last. Nine of twelve slots differ from the rule. STRUCK 2026-09-01: that comparison measures something nobody uses. MEASURED — settings.draftSettings.type is OFFLINE, so ESPN's pickOrder is vestigial: the auction runs on PrimeTime. Owner, 2026-09-01: "ESPN does not store the draft order. We do not use that." The instruction stands and is the part that mattered: do not read nomination order from ESPN. It is generated by python bin/ifv_leagueops.py, and it is the one league-ops generator with no ground truth to backtest against — nothing has ever recorded a nomination order.

Amendment 3 -- the "cannot go below the base" rule took effect in 2023

Part 1 states the rule verbatim: "The additional funds above the base price can be distributed in any form over the life of the contract (i.e., you cannot go below the base)." Part 1 is the 2024 rulebook, and this log did not say when the clause entered it. Seasons 2013-2022 are not governed by it.

This is not a change to the rule. It is the rule's effective date, recorded because the ledger spans 2013-2026 and a validator with no date applies a 2023 rule to 2013.

Source: owner, 2026-08-30 -- asked directly and answered "1. 2023." (docs/DECISIONS.md R127 section 1). He had said the same in passing on 2026-08-28 -- "we changed the rule in 2023, I believe" (R116) -- and that hedged recollection was explicitly NOT treated as a date; this entry exists because he was asked again without the hedge.

Implementation: ifv.validate.checks.NO_BELOW_BASE_FROM_SEASON, applied per ROW in _below_base (a whole-term check has no season to test). Five contracts stop being charged -- 2017 Cooper, 2017 Gronkowski, 2018 Shepard, 2019 Freeman, 2022 A. Jones -- and the five grandfathered dispositions that had been standing in for this date were deleted (R130). grandfathered asserts the league forgave a violation; the truth is that no rule existed to break.

Amendment 4 — the regular season is 14 weeks, not 13

Part 1 says "The regular season runs from Week 1 to Week 13" and, one sentence later, "each team plays the other 3 teams in his division twice … Each team then plays every other team once." Those two sentences cannot both hold: 3 rivals × 2 + 8 other teams × 1 = 14 games.

Source: owner, 2026-09-01 — "It's 14 weeks, the rulebook at 13 weeks is wrong."

Part 1 was not wrong when written; it went stale in 2021. MEASURED over every recorded season:

The 13-week seasons ran 78 games — 13 per team — so each team dropped one inter-division opponent, breaking Part 1's own "every other team once" clause. From 2021 the league runs 84 games — 14 per team — and satisfies the scheduling rule exactly.

Implementation: ifv.leagueops.schedule.FOURTEEN_WEEK_FROM_SEASON = 2021 and intra_weeks_for(season). A generator that hard-coded 13 would emit a schedule that cannot satisfy the rule it implements.

Implementation notes

These are not rules. They are decisions about how the rules are computed, kept here so the reasoning sits next to the text it interprets.

Timestamps. All acquisition times are stored as full ISO-8601 in league time (America/New_York), never UTC-truncated dates. A Sunday 23:00 ET pickup truncates to Monday under UTC, crossing Amendment 1's cutoff and stripping a keeper right that exists. processDate is authoritative for a resolved waiver claim; proposedDate is re-stamped to match it once a batch runs, so it is not a submission time.

Contract validation happens once, at signing — never re-derived annually. A chain containing CUT years is reconstructed from the dead-money bounds (salary ∈ {2d−1, 2d}) against the formula total; a unique solution validates, several report as ambiguous, none is a proven violation.

Roughly 11% of recorded contracts fail the formula. They are logged to rule_exceptions for human adjudication rather than halting a load. The league's real history contains violations; refusing to load them is not an option.

Dispute resolution. Part 1 gives the commissioners sole discretion by consensus. For 2024–25 those were Ray and Peter; as of 2026 the commissioner is Leonard. Rule changes require a simple majority — seven ownership groups — and must be made in the offseason.
